Course Builder

A course-authoring system I designed and built at Smrt English. It helps a small team draft course material from a brief or a syllabus, with educators deciding what ships.

Architect and sole engineer
Built

Claude, Gemini, ElevenLabs, PHP, Python, MySQL

~25k active students

The problem.

Smrt English runs an integrated English curriculum for universities and language schools. The adult program runs from ENG 110 to ENG 145, mapped to CEFR A1 to C1. Building course material by hand was slow and uneven:

  • Teachers and authors wrote lessons in documents, and someone re-entered them into the platform.
  • Media was the bottleneck. Images and audio were stock, inconsistent from unit to unit, or missing.
  • Style, level and exercise quality varied with the author.
  • Matching one university's syllabus, grammar scope and CEFR outcomes by its deadline was hard, so a custom course for a single institution was rarely practical.

The goal: help a small team produce lessons, exercises, images, dialogue audio and assessments that hold one house style and one level from the first unit to the last, with educators deciding what ships.

How it works.

Work moves through phases. In each phase, specialized agents draft one kind of material, each with a fresh context. Automated checks run on what they produce, and a person reviews and approves it before the next phase starts. Each course keeps a state file, so any step can stop and resume.

Claude does the writing and review, Gemini the images and research, and ElevenLabs the voices. House style guides hold the pedagogy every agent follows: lesson structure, exercise formats, variety rules and language-quality rules.

Where a person decides.

The system stops and waits at each of these points. Nothing reaches students without passing all of them.

  • task_altCourse planEducators approve the units, levels and syllabus coverage before anything else is drafted.
  • task_altCharactersThe cast, their looks and their voices are approved once and reused across the course.
  • task_altMaterialsEducators choose the readings and videos each unit uses.
  • task_altFlagged workWhen a reviewer agent isn't sure about something, the build stops and asks a person.
  • task_altSpendExtra generation cost needs a person's OK.
  • task_altEvery unitEducators review and approve every unit. Every note they leave gets a recorded decision.
  • task_altReleaseNothing goes live until a person runs the release step. The agents can't run it.

How it's tested.

Separate reviewers

Work is checked by reviewer agents that don't share the writer's context, so they don't inherit its blind spots.

Scripts that stop the build

Structural problems, such as a broken exercise, missing media or an inconsistent character, stop the build. They don't just log a warning.

Models chosen by blind test

Before switching models for a job, I compare the candidates on blind samples. The default or the newest isn't automatically the best.

Alarms people trust

A check that raises false alarms teaches people to ignore it, so I tune each check until a failure means something.

What broke.

Consistency across a course

Characters, art and voices drifted from unit to unit, and new courses started to resemble old ones. Consistency had to be designed in at the start; checking for it at the end was too late.

Drift from house style

Over long runs, the output got repetitive and slid away from house style. Shorter runs with fresh context, and reviewers that read a whole unit at once, fixed most of it.

Gates that leave no record

A QA step once skipped silently when a build resumed, and nothing showed it hadn't run. A check that passes and a check that never ran should look different.

Automatic fixes

An automatic repair loop for images made builds slower without making them better. Now checks report problems and a person decides what to redo.

What I'd do differently.

  • Evals first. I added most checks after something failed. I'd start with a scored test set built from educators' edits on day one.
  • Consistency first. I designed characters and voices after content was already being written. Designing them first is cheaper.

Both are how the system works now.

My role

I designed and built all of it: the architecture, agents, style guides, checks and release path. A junior developer runs builds and does first-pass QC. Educators review and approve every unit.
